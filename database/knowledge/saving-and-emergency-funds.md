## Build an emergency fund first

An emergency fund is money set aside for unexpected costs such as a medical bill, a sudden repair, or a gap between jobs. A common guideline is to aim for three to six months of essential expenses over time. Start with a smaller first target, such as one month of expenses, so that progress feels achievable.

## Keep emergency money accessible

Emergency savings should be easy to reach quickly and not exposed to large losses, so many people keep them in a separate savings account rather than in investments whose value can fall. Keeping it separate from your everyday account reduces the temptation to spend it.

## How compound growth works

Compounding means earning returns on your previous returns as well as on the money you originally saved. For example, if 10,000 grows by 8 percent in a year, it becomes 10,800; if the next year's 8 percent is earned on 10,800, it becomes 11,664. Over long periods, starting early can matter more than the amount saved, because returns have more time to build. Real returns are not guaranteed and investments can lose value.

## Set specific goals

Saving is easier with specific goals: what the money is for, how much is needed, and by when. Dividing the amount by the number of months gives a monthly saving target you can build into your budget.
