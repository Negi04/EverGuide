## Start with what you already know about yourself

A useful career decision usually starts with three questions: what you enjoy doing, what you are good at or could become good at, and what kind of life you want the work to support. Write short answers to each. Look at subjects, projects, jobs or hobbies where time passed quickly, and at tasks other people regularly ask you to help with. Patterns across these lists are more reliable than a single strong interest.

## Explore before you commit

Treat early choices as experiments rather than permanent decisions. Ways to explore cheaply include reading job descriptions for roles that interest you, watching day-in-the-life videos from people in the role, taking a short online course, volunteering, and doing a small project that resembles the real work. After each experiment, note what energised you and what drained you.

## Talk to people who do the work

Short informational conversations with people a few years into a field are one of the best sources of realistic information. Ask what a typical week looks like, what they wish they had known earlier, which skills mattered most in their first year, and how people usually enter the field. Most people are happy to give twenty minutes if you are specific and respectful of their time.

## Compare options with clear criteria

When you have two or three options, compare them against criteria that matter to you, such as the work itself, learning and growth, entry requirements, cost and time of training, job availability where you want to live, pay over time, and flexibility. Give each criterion a weight. This does not make the decision for you, but it makes trade-offs visible and easier to discuss with family or mentors.

## Make a small, reversible next step

Instead of trying to plan the next ten years, choose the next step that keeps options open and teaches you the most, such as a foundation course, an internship, or a project you can show. Set a date to review how it is going. Many successful careers are built from a series of well-chosen small steps rather than one perfect plan.
