## Write for the specific role

A resume works best when it is tailored to the role you are applying for. Read the job description carefully, identify the most important requirements, and make sure your relevant experience for each one is easy to find. A shorter, focused resume is usually more effective than a long general one.

## Describe achievements, not only duties

Instead of listing responsibilities, describe what you did and what changed because of it. A useful pattern is action, context and result: "Redesigned the weekly report, cutting preparation time from four hours to one." Use numbers where they are honest and meaningful, such as time saved, people helped, or growth achieved.

## Keep the structure simple

Use clear section headings such as summary, experience, projects, education and skills. Simple formatting is easier to read for people and for applicant tracking systems that many employers use to scan applications. Avoid putting important text inside images, tables or unusual layouts that software may not read correctly.

## Include projects and learning when experience is limited

Students and career changers can strengthen a resume with projects, internships, volunteering and relevant coursework. For each project, explain the problem, what you built or did, the tools you used, and the outcome. A link to a portfolio or code repository can help, as long as it is well organised.

## Check before you send

Proofread carefully, ideally after a break or by reading aloud. Check that dates, contact details and links are correct. Ask someone you trust to read it and tell you what they think your target role is; if they cannot tell, the resume may need more focus. Never include false information, because it can end an application or a job later.
