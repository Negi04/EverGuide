## Why sleep matters

Sleep supports memory, concentration, mood and physical health. Most adults function best with roughly seven to nine hours per night, and teenagers generally need more. Needs vary between people, so notice how you feel and function on different amounts of sleep.

## Habits that support better sleep

Keeping regular sleep and wake times, including on weekends, helps the body's internal clock. A wind-down routine without bright screens for the last half hour or so before bed, a dark and quiet room, and avoiding caffeine later in the day help many people. If you cannot sleep, getting up for a short quiet activity and returning when sleepy can be better than lying awake worrying.

## Moving more during the day

Regular physical activity benefits both body and mind. Widely used public health guidance suggests adults aim for around 150 minutes of moderate activity per week, such as brisk walking, plus activities that strengthen muscles on two or more days. Any increase from your current level is worthwhile, and short sessions count.

## Making activity fit a busy schedule

Combine activity with daily routines: walk or cycle for short trips, take stairs, stand and stretch between study or work sessions, or schedule short walks with friends. Choosing activities you enjoy makes them easier to keep up.

## When to check with a professional

Speak to a doctor before starting a new exercise programme if you have a health condition, pain, or symptoms such as chest pain or dizziness. Ongoing sleep problems, loud snoring with pauses in breathing, or daytime sleepiness that affects safety are also good reasons to seek medical advice. This guide is general information, not medical advice.
