## Understand the format first

Technical interviews vary widely. They may include live problem solving, take-home assignments, system or case discussions, or questions about your past projects. Ask the recruiter what format to expect and which skills will be assessed, then prepare for that format rather than everything at once.

## Strengthen fundamentals

For software roles, fundamentals usually include data structures, algorithms, writing clean code and testing. For data roles, they often include SQL, statistics and explaining analysis clearly. Review the fundamentals listed in the job description and practise regularly in short sessions rather than occasional long ones.

## Think aloud and clarify the problem

Interviewers are often as interested in your reasoning as in the final answer. Restate the problem in your own words, ask clarifying questions, discuss a simple approach before optimising, and test your solution with examples, including edge cases. If you get stuck, say what you are thinking; interviewers can often help when they can follow your reasoning.

## Be ready to discuss your projects

Expect detailed questions about projects on your resume. Be able to explain the problem, your design choices, trade-offs you considered, what went wrong, and what you would improve. Only list projects you can discuss confidently.

## Practise under realistic conditions

Do some practice with a timer and without looking up answers, and if possible do mock interviews with a peer or mentor. After each practice session, note what slowed you down and focus the next session on that.
