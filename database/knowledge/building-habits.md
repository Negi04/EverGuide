## Start smaller than you think

New habits are easier to keep when they start very small, such as reading two pages, practising a skill for ten minutes, or walking for five minutes. A small habit done consistently builds the routine; you can increase it once it feels automatic.

## Attach habits to existing routines

Linking a new habit to something you already do every day makes it easier to remember, for example "after I make my morning tea, I will review my goals for the day". Being specific about when and where helps more than relying on motivation.

## Make it easy and visible

Reduce friction for good habits and add friction for unwanted ones. Lay out what you need in advance, keep reminders visible, and remove distractions such as notifications during focused work.

## Track progress, and plan for missed days

A simple tracker, such as marking a calendar, makes progress visible and satisfying. Missing a day is normal; what matters is returning the next day. Plan in advance what you will do on difficult days, such as a shorter version of the habit.

## Review regularly

Every week or two, look at which habits are working and why. Adjust the size, timing or method of habits that keep slipping instead of blaming yourself. Connecting habits to a goal you care about, such as a career milestone, helps keep them meaningful.
