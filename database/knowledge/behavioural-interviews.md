## What behavioural questions are for

Behavioural questions such as "Tell me about a time you handled a conflict" ask for real examples from your past. Interviewers use them because past behaviour is a reasonable guide to how you might act in similar situations. They are listening for what you did, how you thought about it, and what you learned.

## Use the STAR structure

STAR stands for situation, task, action and result. Briefly describe the situation and your responsibility, spend most of the answer on the actions you personally took, and finish with the result and what you learned. Say "I" when describing your own contribution, even in team settings, so the interviewer understands your role.

## Prepare a small set of flexible stories

Prepare five to eight stories from work, study or volunteering that show common qualities: teamwork, leadership, handling a mistake, solving a difficult problem, managing pressure, and dealing with disagreement. One good story can often answer several different questions if you adjust the emphasis.

## Be honest about mistakes

Questions about failure or weakness are a chance to show self-awareness. Choose a real example, take responsibility without excessive self-criticism, and explain specifically what you changed afterwards. Avoid examples that suggest a serious problem with honesty or safety.

## Practise out loud

Practising aloud reveals answers that are too long or unclear. Aim for answers of around one to two minutes, and record yourself or practise with a friend or mentor. Ask for feedback on clarity and whether the result of each story was easy to understand.
