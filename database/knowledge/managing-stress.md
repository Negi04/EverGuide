## Stress is common, and some of it is manageable

Stress is a normal response to pressure such as exams, job searches, deadlines or money worries. Short periods of stress can even help focus. It becomes a problem when it is intense, lasts a long time, or starts affecting sleep, relationships, eating, or the ability to cope with daily life. This guide covers general self-help ideas; it is not a diagnosis or a substitute for professional care.

## Notice your early signs

People experience stress differently. Common signs include trouble sleeping, irritability, difficulty concentrating, tense muscles, headaches, and withdrawing from people. Noticing your own early signs makes it easier to act before stress builds up.

## Practical techniques that many people find helpful

Slow breathing can calm the body: breathe in gently through the nose for about four seconds and out for about six, for a few minutes. Breaking large tasks into small next steps reduces the feeling of being overwhelmed. Regular movement, time outdoors, limiting caffeine late in the day, and keeping a consistent sleep schedule also help many people. Writing down worries, and one small action for each, can make them feel more manageable.

## Stay connected

Talking to someone you trust, such as a friend, family member, mentor or teacher, can reduce stress and help you see a situation more clearly. You do not have to wait until things are very difficult to reach out.

## When to seek professional support

Consider speaking to a doctor, counsellor or other qualified professional if stress or low mood lasts for more than a couple of weeks, if it is affecting daily life, or if you are using alcohol or other substances to cope. If you are thinking about harming yourself or feel unsafe, seek help immediately: contact local emergency services (112 in India) or a crisis helpline such as India's Tele-MANAS service (14416), and tell someone you trust.
